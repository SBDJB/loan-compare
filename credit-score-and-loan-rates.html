How your credit score affects personal loan rates

Lenders charge more when they think a loan is riskier. Your credit score is their main shorthand for that risk, though it is not the only thing they look at.

Why the same loan costs people different amounts

An advertised range such as "8% to 30% APR" covers every borrower a lender approves. Applicants the lender judges most likely to repay are offered rates near the bottom. Those it sees as higher risk are offered rates near the top, or are declined.

What goes into a credit score

The widely used FICO score is built from five kinds of information in your credit report. FICO publishes their approximate weight:

  • Payment history, about 35%. Whether you have paid past accounts on time.
  • Amounts owed, about 30%. Especially how much of your available card credit you are using.
  • Length of credit history, about 15%.
  • New credit, about 10%. Recently opened accounts and recent applications.
  • Credit mix, about 10%. Experience with different kinds of credit.

What else lenders look at

  • Income and employment. Whether you earn enough, reliably, to make the payment.
  • Debt-to-income ratio. Your monthly debt payments as a share of your monthly income. A lower ratio means more room for a new payment.
  • The loan itself. Amount, term and purpose can all move the rate.
  • A co-borrower. Some lenders let a second person with stronger credit apply with you, which can lower the rate. That person becomes equally responsible for the debt.

Ways to get a better offer

  1. Check your credit reports for errors. In the US you can get them free at AnnualCreditReport.com. Dispute anything that is wrong.
  2. Pay down card balances. Lower balances relative to your limits can lift a score fairly quickly.
  3. Pay everything on time in the months before you apply.
  4. Hold off on other new credit until the loan is settled.
  5. Compare several lenders. Lenders weigh the same profile differently, so offers vary. Prequalifying lets you compare without affecting your score.
  6. Borrow only what you need and consider a shorter term if the payment is affordable.

If the offers are all expensive. It may be worth waiting a few months and improving your credit first. A loan at a very high rate can leave you worse off than the problem it was meant to solve.
