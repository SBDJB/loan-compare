Personal loan fees explained

The interest rate is only part of the price. These are the fees to look for in a loan offer and what each one costs you.

Origination fee

An origination fee is a one-time charge for setting up the loan, quoted as a percentage of the amount borrowed. Many lenders charge none. Where one applies, it is often somewhere between 1% and 10%, and tends to be higher for borrowers with weaker credit.

The fee is usually deducted from the money you receive. You still repay, and pay interest on, the full loan amount.

What that means in practice

Suppose you need $10,000 in hand and the lender charges a 6% origination fee.

  • Borrow $10,000 and you receive $9,400. You are $600 short.
  • To receive the full $10,000, you need to borrow about $10,638.30, of which $638.30 goes to the fee.

Either way the fee is real money. It is included in the loan's APR, which is why APR is the right number for comparing a loan that has a fee with one that does not. Our APR guide shows the calculation.

Late payment fee

Charged when a payment arrives after the due date, sometimes after a short grace period. It may be a flat amount or a percentage of the missed payment. A payment that is 30 days or more late can also be reported to the credit bureaus, which costs far more than the fee itself in the long run.

Returned payment fee

Charged if a payment fails, for example because the bank account did not have enough money in it. Your bank may charge its own fee on top.

Prepayment penalty

A charge for paying off the loan ahead of schedule. Many personal loans have no prepayment penalty, which means every extra dollar you pay early saves interest. If a loan does have one, weigh it against how likely you are to pay early.

One thing early repayment does not recover is an origination fee. It was paid upfront and is not refunded, so a fee-heavy loan repaid quickly is expensive for the time you had it.

Less common charges

  • Payment method fees. Some lenders charge for paying by check or by phone.
  • Optional add-ons. Credit insurance or similar products may be offered with the loan. They are optional. Decline anything you did not ask for.

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. Is there an origination fee, and is it taken out of the loan amount?
  2. How much money will actually arrive in my account?
  3. What is the APR, including all fees?
  4. What is the late fee, and is there a grace period?
  5. Can I pay extra or pay the loan off early without a charge?

In the United States, the lender must give you a disclosure showing the APR, the finance charge, the amount financed and the total of payments before you become obligated. Read it and check it against what you were quoted.
