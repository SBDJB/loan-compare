Prequalification versus applying: what each does to your credit

You can usually find out what rate a lender would offer you before you apply, and without any effect on your credit score.

Prequalification

When you prequalify, you give a lender some basic details, typically your income, housing costs and the loan you want, and it shows you the rates and terms you are likely to qualify for.

To do this the lender runs a soft inquiry on your credit. Soft inquiries are visible to you on your credit report but are not seen by other lenders and do not affect your credit score. You can prequalify with as many lenders as you like.

A prequalified offer is an estimate. It is not a commitment to lend.

The formal application

When you choose a lender and apply, it verifies what you told it and runs a hard inquiry. A hard inquiry:

  • can lower your credit score by a few points for a time;
  • stays on your credit report for two years, though scoring models mainly count the most recent twelve months;
  • is visible to other lenders.

One hard inquiry is a small thing. Several in a short period can add up and may suggest to lenders that you are seeking a lot of new credit.

Shopping around the right way

For mortgages, auto loans and student loans, credit scoring models generally treat several inquiries made within a short window as one, so that people can shop for a rate. That treatment generally does not extend to personal loans.

The practical approach for a personal loan is:

  1. Prequalify with several lenders. This costs your score nothing.
  2. Compare the offers on APR, monthly payment, total cost and fees. See how to compare personal loans.
  3. Formally apply to the one lender you have chosen.

Why the final offer can differ

The rate you are approved for can be different from the prequalified estimate if:

  • the income or debts the lender verifies do not match what you entered;
  • your credit report has changed since the soft inquiry;
  • you change the amount or term you ask for.

Entering accurate figures when you prequalify is the best way to make the estimate hold.

What lenders usually ask for

  • Identification, such as a driver's license, and your Social Security number.
  • Proof of income: recent pay stubs, tax returns or bank statements.
  • Proof of address.
  • Bank account details for paying out the loan and collecting payments.

Only give this information on the lender's own secure site, and only to a lender you have checked is legitimate. No genuine lender asks for a fee before paying out a loan.
